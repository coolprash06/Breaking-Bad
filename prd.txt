======================================================================
1. OVERVIEW
======================================================================

An AI early-warning system for water sources in western India's arid
regions (Thar Desert in Rajasthan and Kutch in Gujarat). It predicts
when a water source is likely to fail (run dry or degrade), says how
confident the prediction is, and sends warnings weeks in advance to
the people who depend on that source, in a way that fits how each
group uses the water.

One-line pitch:
"A weather forecast for wells: warn people weeks before their water
runs out, and tell them where to go next."

======================================================================
2. PROBLEM
======================================================================

- Villages, herders and farmers in Thar and Kutch depend on a small
  number of water sources.
- Today, people usually find out a source is failing only when it is
  already dry.
- By then the options are a long journey to another source, buying
  tanker water, or losing livestock and crops.
- Different people use the same source in very different ways, so
  one generic alert does not help everyone.

======================================================================
3. GOALS (mapped to the official CORE)
======================================================================

G1. Take satellite and/or sensor data (real, public, or realistically
    simulated) about water sources.
G2. Predict each source's future (e.g. days until dry, expected
    level) WITH a confidence level.
G3. Turn the prediction into a warning people can act on, weeks
    before the crisis.
G4. Show the full chain working end to end:
    data -> prediction -> warning received.

Success for the demo:
- At least one source shown going from Safe to Warning, with a
  warning visibly received (simulated phone screen + real SMS).
- Clear statement of which data is real and which is simulated.

======================================================================
4. USERS AND PERSONAS
======================================================================

Personas are grouped into 4 categories by HOW they use the water.
Each category draws water at a different rate (used by the model)
and needs a different warning (used by the alert system).

CATEGORY 1 - FETCH AND CARRY AWAY
- Women collecting household drinking/cooking water from stepwells,
  wells, ponds and tanks
- Water tanker operators supplying other villages
Needs: which source is still safe and how far it is.

CATEGORY 2 - USE IT ON THE SPOT
- Herders watering livestock at the source
- People bathing or washing clothes at the source
- Travellers / pilgrims stopping at the source
Needs: where to move (especially herds) and by when.

CATEGORY 3 - PUMP IT OUT AT SCALE
- Farmers using borewells / tubewells for irrigation
Needs: how much to cut pumping to keep shared sources alive longer.

CATEGORY 4 - MANAGE AND OVERSEE
- Village Panchayat: directs farmers and citizens on water use;
  sees all nearby sources on a dashboard
- Escalation from Panchayat to local MLA / MP when problems are
  beyond village level
- District water officer (water authority managing many sources)
- NGOs / aid agencies deciding where to send water tankers
Needs: overview map, who is affected, and the ability to act or
escalate.

No single primary persona. The app is organised around the 4
purpose categories (see Section 8A). Each category's screen lists
the personas/stories under it.

======================================================================
5. WATER SOURCES IN SCOPE
======================================================================

All water sources possible in the region, including:
- Open dug wells
- Borewells / tubewells
- Stepwells (baoris)
- Village ponds (nadis)
- Covered rainwater tanks (tankas)
- Shallow fresh-water pits (beris in Thar, virdas in Kutch)
  [verify these local terms before using in the pitch]
- Oases / natural water bodies, where present

Each source has a "type" field, because each type behaves
differently (e.g. ponds depend on rain and evaporation; borewells
depend on groundwater).

7. DATA
======================================================================

Rule: every dataset is labelled REAL or SIMULATED in the app and in
the judges' notes.

REAL (public):
- CGWB groundwater levels via India-WRIS (observation wells, measured
  about 4 times a year; some stations have automatic recorders)
- Rainfall: CHIRPS (via Google Earth Engine) or IMD
- Vegetation greenness (NDVI) around sources: Sentinel-2 (via Google
  Earth Engine) - optional, as a supporting signal

SIMULATED (clearly labelled):
- Daily sensor readings between the real quarterly measurements
- Usage per source, by persona category (households, herds,
  bathing, farm pumping)
- Sensor failures and gaps (to demonstrate messy-data handling)

Simulation must be realistic: levels drop faster in summer heat and
with heavy use, rise slightly after rare rainfall, and have gaps.

8A. MAIN SCREEN LAYOUT (4 PURPOSE TABS)
======================================================================

Top of the main screen: 4 tabs, one per purpose category:
  [Fetch & Carry] [Use on Spot] [Pump at Scale] [Manage & Oversee]

Clicking a tab changes the dashboard to that purpose's view.

Left/main area: shared map + dashboard, changing per tab:
- Fetch & Carry: nearest safe sources, walking distance
- Use on Spot: herd routes, which sources can handle animals
- Pump at Scale: farm borewells, how much pumping to cut
- Manage & Oversee: full overview, everyone affected, escalate to
  MLA / MP

Side panel: the personas / stories under the selected purpose
(e.g. Use on Spot -> herder, people bathing, travellers). Clicking
a persona shows their story and the exact warning they receive.

Build rule (to save time): ONE shared map, ONE shared data layer
and ONE shared forecast engine. Tabs only change:
- which sources are highlighted
- the info panel content
- the warning message style
(One engine, four "lenses" - not four separate apps.)

8. FEATURES
======================================================================

MUST-HAVE (covers the core)

F1. Map of water sources (Thar + Kutch)
    - Marker colour = RISK level:
        Green  = Safe
        Yellow = Watch (dropping faster than usual)
        Orange = Warning (likely to fail in weeks)
        Red    = Emergency (act now)
        Grey   = No recent data (sensor dead / gap)
    - Confidence shown SEPARATELY from colour (e.g. faded vs solid
      marker, and a label like "~5 weeks left, 70% confident")
    - Filter by source type and by region

F2. Source detail panel (on click)
    - Level chart: past values + forecast + shaded confidence band
    - "Likely to fail in X-Y weeks" estimate
    - Reasons ("No rain for 60 days, usage up 30%")
    - Who uses this source (by persona category)
    - Which warnings have been sent and to whom

F3. Forecast model
    - Predicts future level and time until failure per source
    - Outputs a confidence range, not a single number
    - Uses usage by persona category as an input
    - Model choice: [OPEN] (e.g. Prophet or Random Forest)

F4. Alert engine (false-alarm vs missed-alarm balance)
    - Tiered levels (Watch / Warning / Emergency)
    - Persistence check: signal must hold for several readings
    - Multiple signals must agree before escalating (e.g. level
      dropping + no rain + vegetation browning)
    - Confidence stated in every alert

F5. Persona-specific warnings
    - Different message per persona category, for example:
      Cat 1: "Well #4 low. Well #7 (2 km) is safe."
      Cat 2: "Move herds to Well #7 within 2 weeks."
      Cat 3: "Reduce pumping 30% to keep the village well alive
              6 more weeks."
      Cat 4: full picture + who is affected
    - "Nearest safe source" suggestion in every field warning

F6. Warning delivery
    - Real SMS (Twilio trial) to team phones in the demo
    - Simulated basic-phone screen inside the web app as backup
    - Languages: [OPEN] (e.g. Hindi, Gujarati, English)

F7. Messy data handling
    - Sensor gaps shown as Grey, never hidden
    - Forecast widens its confidence band when data is missing
    - Cloudy satellite images skipped, last good value used and
      labelled

F8. Panchayat dashboard (behind existing login page)
    - Overview of all sources in their area
    - Send directions to farmers / citizens
    - "Escalate to MLA / MP" action with an auto-generated summary
      of the issue

SHOULD-HAVE (if time allows)
F9.  Herder / citizen SMS replies to confirm or correct a reading
     (e.g. reply 1 = low, 2 = fine)
F10. Voice call alerts for people who cannot read
F11. Backtest panel: "On past data, the model warned X weeks early,
     with Y false alarms and Z missed alarms"

NICE-TO-HAVE
F12. Rainfall overlay on the map
F13. Tanker routing suggestion for NGOs / aid agencies

9. USER ROLES AND LOGIN
======================================================================

- Field users (Categories 1-3): NO login. They only receive SMS /
  voice warnings on basic phones.
- Category 4 users (Panchayat, district officer, NGO): log in to
  the dashboard using the existing login page.
- A "Try demo" option or demo credentials for judges (put in the
  judges' notes).

======================================================================
10. DESERT RULES (how we respect each one)
======================================================================

R1. Sparse and messy data
    -> F7 (gaps shown, confidence widens, cloudy images skipped)
R2. Basic phones and patchy coverage
    -> SMS/voice only for field users, short messages, no app or
       internet needed on their side
R3. False alarm vs missed alarm
    -> F4 (tiers, persistence, multi-signal agreement, stated
       confidence) + F11 (backtest numbers) if time allows
